    Cajanus cajan (pigeon pea)

        Pigeon pea - has been used in agriculture for a long time. In India, it has been cultivated for more than 3.5 thousand years, and is believed to have been originated there. Nowadays, pigeon pea is grown in many countries around the world. It grows annually on 5 million hectares, in Asia, Africa and South America. But the major area of ??this crop (up to 80%) is in India. India is also a major buyer of pigeon peas seeds in the world market, as its own grop does not meet the needs of this country. Interest to pigeon pea is also increasing in European countries and America, where it is often used in diet, and its consumption is increasing due to the steady increase in immigrants.

        

As for biological features, pigeon pea is an annual or perennial plant. Industrial cultivation uses its annual varieties, whereas perennial species are mainly grown on domestic plots for food and decorative purposes. Pigeon pea plants are well developed, with an average of 1 m in height in annual species and up to 2 -3 m in perennial species. They also have a well-developed stem and root that penetrates into the ground to a depth of 2 m or more. The stem at the bottom is tree-shaped, well-branched. The leaves are alternate and trifoliate. Leaves are oblong and lanceolate, 5-10 cm long and 2-4 cm wide. The leaves and stems are pubescent, which in combination with the developed root system allows to effective resisting the drought. The flowers are grouped into bunched inflorescences (5 to 10) on the tips or axils of the branches. The flowers are butterfly and mostly yellow. They can also be striped with purple stripes. Corolla is about 2-2.5 cm. The fruits are the beans - flat, straight and pubescent, 5-9 cm long and 12-13 mm wide. The bean contains 2-9 seeds, brown, red, white or black.

 

 

        

Pigeon pea flowers are quite attractive to bees and other insects. Therefore, despite the fact that pigeon pea, like other legumes, is a self-pollinated plant – cross-pollination here happens quite often up to 40%. Due to its peculiarity, pigeon pea is still the only legume crop in which has been able to create hybrid varieties.

 

Pigeon pea honey has a greenish tinge and is similar to acacia honey. Due to the fact that the flowering of pigeon pea takes place in the second half of July in early August, when most of the sunflower has already faded, there is a significant prospect of its use for prolonging the period of honey harvest, especially in the southern regions of Ukraine.

 

The seeds vary in size, depending on the variety weight of 1000 seeds ranges from 50 to 150 g, the color of the seed coat can be white, red, brown and other colors

    
  

Pigeon pea is grown mainly for the sake of seeds that are edible. However, it can also be used for many other purposes. Stems of pigeon pea are a good source of fuel, as it grows quickly and develops a good green mass. Stems and branches of pigeon pea are also used for weaving. Among other applications of pigeon pea tests have shown the possibility of using it as raw material for making paper. Cajanus cajan has numerous uses in animal feeding. Leaves and pods are a valuable and high-calorie protein feed. Leaves are sometimes used to replace alfalfa in ruminant diets. By-products of seed processing, and sometimes whole seeds, are used as animal feed. As mentioned, bees actively feed on pigeon pea and produce honey with a characteristic color (greenish).


The region of origining of pigeon pea is considered to be North-East Africa or India. Its cultivation history is at least 3000 years old. Today, pigeon pea can be found in both hemispheres from 30 ° C to 30 ° latitude, and from sea level to 2000 m (3000 m in Venezuela). Due to the fact that it is very heat-resistance pigeon pea it grows better and develops in places where the temperature fluctuates from 20 ° to 40 ° C. Despite the sensitivity to frost, the pea-pigeon does not stop growth and development at temperatures close to 0 ° C, and adult plants can tolerate even mild frost. For high yields, it is better when the annual rainfall exceeds 625 mm, but it is also very drought resistant. Pigeon peas can grow on a wide range of soils - from sands to heavy black clays, with variable pH. However, the best pH is in the range of 5-7. It has a low tolerance to soil salinity, although some varieties have been reported to tolerate high (6-12 dS / m) salinity.
The global production of pigeon pea in 2014 amounted to 4.85 million tons. The main producers were India (3.29 million tons, 65% of world production), Myanmar (0.57 million tons), Malawi (0.3 million tonnes), Kenya (0.28 million tonnes) and Tanzania (0.25 million tonnes). Most of it is grown in Asia (79.1%), followed by Africa (17.6%) and America (2.5%) (FAO).


Growing in Ukraine

To investigate the possibility of growing Cajanus cajan in Ukraine, as well as its growth and development characteristics in our climatic conditions in 2017, 81 specimens of pigeon pea genotypes were obtained from the ICRISAT International Institute for Semi-Deserted Tropics) located in India.
In 2017, the reproduction of the obtained varieties was carried out, as well as a primary assessment of their ability to grow and develop in our climatic conditions. The studies are conducted in the Krasnograd district of Kharkiv region. In 2017, the average annual temperature was 9.7 ° C, and during the year there were only 444 mm of precipitation, of which only 69.9 mm fell in the summer. Under these weather conditions, pigeon pea plants grew and developed quite well. Sowing was conducted on May 3, as were beans in the region. At this time the weather was warm enough for the growth and development of the plants, the seedlings were received on May 9. In the second decade of May, the average daily temperature was only 11 ° C much cooler as ussual, and on certain days the soil temperature dropped to 1 ° C, which to some extent delayed the growth and development of pigeon pea plants. Flowering in the earliest specimens began June 28 - 50 days after emergence. But most of the specimens bloomed after July 20, with some specimens blooming on September 9, and some did not bloom at all. Despite the arid weather during the summer, pigeon pea plants grew quite well and developed an average plant height of 1.1 m, and some reached the height of human growth up to 1.8 m on September 10. Most samples matured in September.
According to preliminary estimates, 49 samples, in which blossoms started as early as possible and were ripening by the end of September, were left for further study.
In 2018, a more detailed study of leftover pigeon pea samples on productivity and other economic-value indicators was conducted.
The average annual temperature this year was 9.5 ° С, the precipitation amount is 551.5 mm, of which 122 mm during the summer.
The sowing this year was held on April 29, the seedlings were received on May 12. The weather at the beginning of vegetative period this year's was slightly warmer than last. The sum of active temperatures at the end of the first decade of July was at 802 ° C, compared to 690.0 °C during this period last year. Accordingly, the flowering of most of the studied pigeon pea specimens this year began almost 10 days earlier. The weather during this period was wet and warm, 80 mm of precipitation fell in July, which contributed to the active growth and development of plants.

Ripening in most samples occurred in mid-September, so the growing season ranged from 108 to 135 days. Yield ranged from 10 to 27 c/ha. The most yielded was the sample 14924 - 27.5 c/ha (Table 1), the vegetation period was 123 days, the average height of the plants is 90 cm, the seeds is average, the mass of 1000 seeds is 85 g. The most productive plant yielded up to 35 g of seeds, and in the less productive it was only 12 g of seeds from the plant.
It should also be noted sample 14407 with a yield of 24.6 c/ha, which had very large seeds, a mass of 1000 seeds of 102 g, and a growing season of only 108 days (ripening on August 28). Sample 14921 is also characterized by a relatively high yield of 23.1 c/ha, as well as having a large vegetative mass, plants up to 168 cm high, but its growing season was 129 days.
The seeds of most of the samples tested were brown in different shades, but there were also samples with white seeds.
Thus, according to the available data, it is already possible to draw some conclusions. Pigeon pea (cajanus cajan) grows and develops quite well in our climatic conditions, showing the yield potential not worse than in the regions of its traditional cultivation. Pigeon pea plants are erect and have a strong stem, and its pods are not prone to cracking so it is well suited for combining. Therefore, this plant has prospects for distribution, especially in light of climate change towards hotter and drier.
